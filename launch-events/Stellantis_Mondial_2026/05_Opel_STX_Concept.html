Opel
STX Concept

An electric estate study that links cargo-friendly volume with a low aerodynamic silhouette. STX advances Opel’s Vizor and Compass language while exploring moving rear aero elements and a more efficient technical exterior.

01 · Design Highlights

The estate is treated as an expressive and useful body type.

The silhouette keeps utility within a low body · Opel · Official imagery
SPORTS TOURER

The silhouette keeps utility within a low body

STX combines a long estate roof with a low, stretched body and short overhangs. The wheel-arch volume gives it a broad stance while the rear remains recognisably organised around carrying space. A practical body type becomes emotionally appealing without SUV height; the photograph does not establish cargo capacity or drag performance.

A larger technical face gives the estate its own scale · Opel · Official imagery
NEW VIZOR

A larger technical face gives the estate its own scale

Opel makes STX's Vizor higher and more prominent than the ultra-slim faces common on many electric concepts. The central illuminated Blitz and vertical Compass axis organise the broad front around one stable reference. The face gains scale and identity through organised landmarks; actual lamp visibility remains an optical-engineering question.

A carrying envelope stays visible from above · Opel · Official imagery
ROOF AND REAR

A carrying envelope stays visible from above

The overhead view reveals a continuous roof volume rather than an aggressively shortened passenger-and-cargo envelope. Opel describes a steep rear that preserves useful luggage space while maintaining an aerodynamic overall silhouette. Carrying is part of the exterior brief, although exact luggage dimensions and loading clearances are not provided.

A show vehicle makes the exterior proposal tangible · Quattroruote
PHYSICAL STUDY

A show vehicle makes the exterior proposal tangible

Quattroruote's photographed presentation places the STX concept beside a person, giving its broad, low body a useful human reference. This moves the exterior discussion beyond isolated studio imagery, while Opel states that the interior remains a future development direction. Exterior intent is tangible, but proposed cabin lighting and seats must remain identified as future work rather than completed STX hardware.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Airflow, wheel design and cabin research follow explicit functions.

Moving elements adapt to the driving situation · Opel · Official imagery
ACTIVE REAR AERO

Moving elements adapt to the driving situation

Opel describes extending rear diffusers and lateral bumper elements that adjust airflow as conditions change. The moving geometry is intended to reduce drag and improve directional stability rather than serving only as visual theatre. The photograph locates the aero treatment; without a published drag-reduction figure, its benefit remains a manufacturer concept claim.

A wheel face participates in the aero package · Opel · Official imagery
THREE-ZONE WHEELS

A wheel face participates in the aero package

STX uses aerodynamically treated three-section wheels with 285/35 R22 Goodyear tyres, according to Opel. The broad closed regions and controlled openings visually organise the rotating component as part of the airflow strategy. The detail links technical appearance to function; cooling, mass and consumption effects require evaluation within the complete vehicle system.

Airflow is considered at the body boundary · Opel · Official imagery
FLUSH SURFACING

Airflow is considered at the body boundary

Opel emphasises flush, precisely shaped surfaces as part of STX's efficiency-oriented exterior. The close body view shows controlled transitions around the glazing and upper panels, reducing the visual interruption of the main volume. Joints and protrusions become architectural choices, while a smooth-looking panel alone cannot establish actual airflow performance.

Future STX interior work has an explicit predecessor · Opel · 2023 Experimental context
CABIN RESEARCH LINEAGE

Future STX interior work has an explicit predecessor

Opel says STX's future cabin will develop immersive lighting and lightweight seating ideas from the 2023 Experimental and later concepts. The illustrated Experimental cabin is a contextual reference, showing the earlier integration of light and reduced interior mass. The predecessor explains the research path without inventing an STX cabin; new controls and seating geometry remain unpublished.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

Lamellas, glass strips and precise axes organise the surfaces.

A dense light pattern retains a central hierarchy · Opel · Official imagery
LAMELLA RHYTHM

A dense light pattern retains a central hierarchy

STX's front signature uses slanted vertical lamellas that gradually fade toward the centre. The illuminated emblem and Compass axis remain the primary anchors, preventing the repeated strips from becoming an entirely uniform field. An ordered focus makes the dense pattern read as precise, rather than assuming that decorative animation universally improves recognition.

The rear quarter gets a material-led accent · Opel · Official imagery
GLASS STRIPS

The rear quarter gets a material-led accent

Glass light strips extend from STX's window line toward the rear and continue into the roof, according to Opel. Their narrow geometry contrasts with the broader sculpted body and makes the C-pillar transition a distinct technical feature. The narrow accent identifies the upper body without heavy ornament; hidden optical construction and durability are not revealed.

A small luminous word belongs to the surface system · Opel · Official imagery
STX IDENTIFICATION

A small luminous word belongs to the surface system

Illuminated STX lettering is integrated into the concept's sharply defined side treatment. The narrow label and blue accent relate to the surrounding technical geometry rather than appearing as a large unrelated badge. Variant identity stays within the shared surface language, while operational feedback still needs distinct and understandable status cues.

The new face evolves an existing architecture · Opel · 2023 Experimental context
VIZOR LINEAGE

The new face evolves an existing architecture

The contextual 2023 Opel Experimental image shows an earlier Vizor and Compass arrangement, rather than the new STX. Comparing this lineage with STX's larger face and lamella treatment reveals a stable central structure with evolving proportions and light graphics. Continuity through organised landmarks allows more expressive new products without requiring an unrelated identity system.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Four concrete experiments for Zebra products.

01 · Make surface decisions support a task

Review protrusions, seams and recesses around scanners and cradles as airflow, contamination and cleaning decisions, inspired by STX's flush exterior. Test ingress paths and wipe access before accepting smoother styling.

02 · Tie moving parts to an operating objective

Require each adjustable accessory feature to improve carrying, access or attachment, using active aero's functional logic as a reference. Measure benefit against mechanism wear, accidental activation and recovery steps.

03 · Keep one primary feedback landmark

Use a stable spatial anchor for charging and scan status across product sizes, borrowing the Compass axis. Evaluate peripheral recognition and mounting-orientation changes before adding decorative light sequences.

04 · Preserve useful volume when shaping hardware

Compare cradle and holster envelopes against insertion clearance and carried volume, following STX's cargo-conscious estate proportions. Prioritise glove access and snag-free removal over dramatic surfaces that consume working space.

06 · Key Takeaway

An emotional estate still has to earn its shape through usable space and efficiency.

STX’s strongest argument connects a steep rear volume with active aero and an aligned identity system. Zebra can transfer that discipline to rugged housings and accessories: expressive geometry should support carrying, airflow, visibility or attachment rather than consume useful space without a task benefit.
