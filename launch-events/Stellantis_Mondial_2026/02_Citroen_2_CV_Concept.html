Citroën
2 CV Concept

A small electric concept that turns affordability into a design discipline. Separate impact pieces, a reduced dashboard and portable everyday objects reinterpret the 2 CV’s useful simplicity for contemporary mobility.

Citroën 2 CV Concept · Auto Express
01 · Design Highlights

An accessible car can have a distinctive, reassuring personality.

A small footprint keeps a recognisable silhouette · Auto Express
COMPACT CHARACTER

A small footprint keeps a recognisable silhouette

The 3.76-metre concept keeps the arched roof and separate round lamps associated with the original 2 CV. These landmarks make the smaller electric package familiar while tauter surfaces give it a contemporary presence. Citroën targets approximately €15,000 for a European-built model by 2028; those ambitions remain distinct from a final production price or specification.

The bumper announces its separate job · Le Nouvel Automobiliste
REPAIR BOUNDARY

The bumper announces its separate job

The front bumper reads as an independent impact element rather than disappearing into a continuous painted body. Citroën states that bumpers are separated from the bodywork and can be replaced independently, giving damage a clearer component boundary. The exposed division makes a service strategy visually understandable; the manufacturer does not publish actual replacement cost.

Removing dashboard bulk leaves room for people · Auto Express
SPATIAL REDUCTION

Removing dashboard bulk leaves room for people

A slim cylinder replaces the conventional dashboard mass, with steering and essential controls attached to it. Recessed door panels and the absence of a large centre console reduce the number of permanent volumes around occupants. The reduced permanent bulk creates perceived openness, while the photograph cannot establish measured legroom or passenger accommodation.

Everyday comfort is given a visible invitation · Auto Express
SEATING

Everyday comfort is given a visible invitation

Wide seats with bright tartan upholstery make the cabin appear welcoming rather than austere. Citroën pairs this visual warmth with its Advanced Comfort seating approach and a pull-out front seat for breaks. Rest becomes part of everyday mobility; the photo shows upholstery, while comfort and the pull-out function are manufacturer-described.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Simplification changes parts, interfaces and everyday objects.

Essential data stays close to the sight line · Auto Express
SMART VISION

Essential data stays close to the sight line

Citroën Smart Vision places key driving information in a slim display near the base of the windscreen. Its position separates persistent status from the eight-inch touch interface used for additional interaction. The hierarchy distinguishes persistent status from requested detail, without assuming that this placement guarantees shorter driver glances.

A small interface has distinct physical neighbours · Auto Express
PHYGITAL CONTROLS

A small interface has distinct physical neighbours

The square touchscreen sits beside a single-spoke steering control and simple lever-based inputs. Separating visual interaction from physically locatable controls gives the driver more than one way to recognise a function. Fewer competing interfaces can remain expressive, provided the comic-inspired graphics do not obscure essential task information.

An object can keep working outside the vehicle · Citroën · Official imagery
RADIOËN

An object can keep working outside the vehicle

Radioën is a detachable radio that also serves as a portable speaker, rather than being permanently hidden in the dashboard. Its recognisable object form and removable mounting let the same function accompany users inside and outside the cabin. One object extends utility across locations; retention, charging and weather protection remain undisclosed engineering details.

A small fragile load gets a dedicated home · Le Nouvel Automobiliste
TASK-SPECIFIC STORAGE

A small fragile load gets a dedicated home

The door compartment holds eggs, a playful reference to the original 2 CV’s practical design brief. A dedicated small-item pocket gives a fragile everyday load a recognisable location rather than treating storage as undifferentiated volume. The dedicated space makes a practical brief concrete, while retention during travel cannot be established from a staged photograph.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

Orange energy, yellow tartan and visible functional boundaries.

A warm exterior energises the small volume · Auto Express
INFRA RED

A warm exterior energises the small volume

The Infra Red finish uses an orange base with yellow and red highlights, according to Citroën. Across the bonnet and rounded wings, the warm hue reinforces the concept's cheerful presence and makes its separate lamps easy to locate visually. The warm finish creates optimistic everyday character; its apparent friendliness is separate from lighting optics and safety performance.

Pale controls break up the yellow cabin · Auto Express
BAKELITE REFERENCES

Pale controls break up the yellow cabin

Beige controls and steering surfaces stand out against the yellow interior, recalling the visual warmth of Bakelite. Their lighter tone establishes a different material family from upholstery and textured panels, helping the control area remain visually identifiable. The nostalgic reference becomes a functional visual contrast, without establishing that every control is operable without looking.

A modest wheel becomes an identity carrier · Auto Express
STEEL AND COLOUR CLIPS

A modest wheel becomes an identity carrier

The concept uses steel wheels with small yellow identification clips rather than a complex sculpted alloy appearance. Simple repeated openings and a concentrated colour detail keep the wheel visually related to the car's economical, practical character. A simple component carries personality through limited detail; actual manufacturing cost and aerodynamic benefit are not provided.

A visible handle keeps the action understandable · Auto Express
TOUCHABLE LANDMARKS

A visible handle keeps the action understandable

The contoured exterior door handle remains a distinct object against the orange body. Its shape and pale finish make the opening action legible in a way that a fully hidden handle would not provide visually. The clear hand target makes the opening action approachable, while wet-hand and glove performance still need evaluation.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Four concrete experiments for Zebra products.

01 · Draw repair boundaries deliberately

Prototype independently replaceable impact parts at terminal corners and cradle contact zones, inspired by the concept's separate bumpers. Measure replacement time, sealing continuity and fastener access after repeated service cycles.

02 · Separate essential status from optional detail

Keep scan result, battery and exceptions persistent while placing secondary functions behind a compact task interface, following Smart Vision's hierarchy. Compare gaze shifts and missed status under bright warehouse lighting.

03 · Let accessories travel with the task

Explore a removable utility module that retains its function when detached, taking Radioën as a reference. Check retention force, charging contacts and contamination before adding portability to a docked component.

04 · Simplify around the user's belongings

Review holster and arm-mount storage as removable containers rather than permanent product bulk, borrowing the basket's logic. Observe loading errors, occupied volume and one-handed retrieval with actual carried objects.

06 · Key Takeaway

Affordability becomes desirable when simplification preserves useful freedom.

The 2 CV Concept combines a compact footprint with clear repair boundaries, essential information and removable objects. Zebra can apply this thinking by reducing permanent hardware and concentrating functions around real field tasks, while preserving service access and dependable status feedback.
