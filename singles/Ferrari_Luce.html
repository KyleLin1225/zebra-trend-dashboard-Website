Design Trend Report · 28 SEP 2026

Ferrari Luce

Ferrari’s first fully electric car turns the four-door, five-seat brief into a tactile grand tourer. Its restrained body and physical driving controls show how an EV can carry a distinct identity beyond screens and acceleration.

01 · Design Highlights

A low, clean body makes a five-seat Ferrari feel singular.

Red Luce front fascia and slim lamp signature · Ferrari official gallery
FRONT IDENTITY

A quiet face with a concealed light signature

Ferrari trades an overt grille for a low continuous front volume. The narrow lamp graphic lets the body form lead when the lights are off.

FOUR-DOOR FORM

A grand tourer stretched around five occupants

The long roof, short overhangs and sculpted sill give the four-door car a recognizable Ferrari proportion without a combustion-engine hood.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Electric performance is mediated through deliberate physical controls.

Steering wheel with physical controls · Ferrari official gallery
TACTILE HMI

Keep frequent driving actions under the fingers

The steering wheel uses discrete buttons and rotary controls around the display. Ferrari and LoveFrom give high-attention tasks a physical target.

Metal paddle controls behind the steering wheel · Ferrari official gallery
DRIVER FEEDBACK

Paddles give EV torque behavior a familiar gesture

Metal paddles retain an immediate mechanical feel. In a prototype drive, MotorTrend describes their role in managing regeneration and torque response.

Red steering wheel drive-mode rotary control · Ferrari official gallery
MODE SELECTION

A visible control makes state changes deliberate

The red rotary control is a distinct touchpoint, separating dynamic mode choice from a buried touchscreen menu.

Rear cabin console vents and physical controls · Ferrari official gallery
SHARED CABIN

Rear passengers receive real control surfaces

The rear center console places vents and local physical controls within reach. A five-seat electric Ferrari is designed as a shared cabin, not only a driver cockpit.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Take the tactile logic into products used under real work constraints.

01 / GLOVED INPUT

Give critical modes a physical target

Translate the wheel’s differentiated rotary and button shapes into gloves-on scanner controls. Validate tactile identification, accidental presses and reach while moving.

02 / STATE FEEDBACK

Make mode changes visible and felt

Pair any operational mode selector with a clear state indication and immediate feedback. Test the behavior under vibration and high ambient noise.

03 / SHARED WORKSPACE

Design for each user position

Use the rear-console lesson in multi-operator carts and vehicle-mounted devices: place local controls where each worker actually stands or sits.

04 / SYSTEM IDENTITY

Carry material cues across objects

Coordinate handheld unit, dock, vehicle mount and wearable accessory with durable, cleanable touchpoints and consistent identity at multiple scales.

06 · Key Takeaway

Make the essential interaction unmistakable to the hand.

Luce pairs an unusually clean EV exterior with differentiated buttons, paddles and rotary controls for high-attention actions. For Zebra, the transferable lesson is to make key states and controls legible by touch and feedback across a rugged device system, then test that clarity with gloves, motion and shared use.
