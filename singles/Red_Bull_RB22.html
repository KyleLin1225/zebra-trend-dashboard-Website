01 · Design Highlights

Make the occupied core and the functional envelope easy to read.

Vladimir Rys / Red Bull Racing · Let function define the silhouette
Functional envelope

Let function define the silhouette

The track photograph makes the exposed wheels, narrow central body and separate aerodynamic surfaces easy to read. RB22 is a 2026 chassis powered by the team’s DM01 unit. Design interpretation: preserve a clear hierarchy between the occupied core and the functional elements around it, rather than treating everything as one sculpted shell.

Vladimir Rys / Red Bull Racing · Keep the occupied zone unmistakable
Spatial hierarchy

Keep the occupied zone unmistakable

The overhead pit-lane view distinguishes the driver cell from the front-wing and wheel envelope. This is a spatial observation, not proof of visibility or comfort. For a compact work tool, separating grip, sensing and protected display zones can make orientation easier to understand before the interface is even activated.

Vladimir Rys / Red Bull Racing · A contact edge deserves its own design
Protected boundary

A contact edge deserves its own design

The close photograph isolates the cockpit rim and adjacent body surface; it does not reveal the steering-wheel interface. The visual lesson is the importance of the boundary around an occupied zone. Zebra can explore similarly deliberate protective edges around displays, grips and mounting interfaces while checking reach and cleaning access.

Oracle Red Bull Racing / Red Bull Content Pool · Use graphics to organize a complex body
Surface zoning

Use graphics to organize a complex body

The official top-view livery render places the central blue field between dark wheels and bright nose and engine-cover graphics. It is launch artwork, not a verified race specification. Design interpretation: distribute identity across major zones so a complicated product can remain coherent from more than one viewing direction.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

The important interfaces sit between hardware, energy and people.

Red Bull / via autosport web · Design the interfaces between systems
Integrated power unit

Design the interfaces between systems

The DM01 workshop photograph, credited to Red Bull and published by autosport web, provides an actual hardware reference beyond exterior styling. The team identifies a 1.6-litre V6 architecture. Ford describes work spanning combustion, charge-air and energy-recovery components: the transferable issue is coordinating subsystem boundaries, rather than copying a racing engine’s packaging.

Oracle Red Bull Racing / Red Bull Content Pool · Moving hardware needs a clear state model
Changing states

Moving hardware needs a clear state model

The front-view launch render exposes the layered wing geometry, while Red Bull’s announcement places RB22 within the new active-aerodynamics era. The render does not demonstrate actuation or race geometry. For Zebra, any moving shutter, dock latch or adjustable mount should have a recognizable state, a defined transition and a predictable failure response.

Vladimir Rys / Red Bull Racing · Design for the people around the product
Operational architecture

Design for the people around the product

The pit-lane photograph shows team members moving RB22 together, with the front envelope and handling equipment sharing a constrained space. It supports a workflow observation, not a measured pit-stop claim. Assembly and service design should include hands, tools, approach directions and temporary supports as part of the product architecture.

Getty Images / Red Bull Content Pool · Connect simulation to real operation
Validation loop

Connect simulation to real operation

The Bahrain garage photograph supplies test context, rather than evidence of a particular internal component. Ford’s powertrain chief engineer describes controls modeling, driver simulator feedback and coordinated battery-energy work. Zebra can apply that loop to scanner latency, thermal behavior and battery management: model the system, then verify it through representative operator tasks.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

The blue body detail shows a patterned surface becoming visible under directional light. Its effect depends on both graphic scale and finish; it should not be read as exposed structural fiber. For Zebra CMF, a restrained pattern could differentiate accessory families, provided contrast, wear and cleaning do not compete with essential markings.

The frontal nose detail combines a large driver number with smaller sponsor marks, framed by dark structural elements. The number remains a prominent anchor within a dense field. 05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Transfer the system logic into clearer states, service tasks and product families.

01

Make operating states explicit

For dock latches, scanner shutters and accessory locks, pair physical position with a consistent visual or tactile cue. Test transitions, partial engagement and failure recovery with gloves; a moving mechanism should never leave the operator guessing whether it is ready.

02

Separate identification from task feedback

Keep serial, model and accessory-family markings in stable zones. Reserve the strongest operational contrast for scan confirmation, connection state and warnings. Evaluate legibility in motion, under glare and after abrasion, rather than assuming a bright color is sufficient.

03

Prototype service as a complete task

Use the pit-lane workflow as a prompt to model technicians, tools and supports around a device. Time battery, trigger and cradle replacement with realistic gloves and space constraints. Preserve seal inspection and access to fasteners while reducing avoidable repositioning.

04

Validate energy behavior with operator tasks

Link thermal and battery models to repeated scanning, wireless communication and docking cycles. Measure latency and predictable low-energy behavior in representative environments. Ford’s simulation-to-test process is a development reference; Zebra’s targets must be established through its own task data.

06 · Key Takeaway

Make complexity legible at the boundaries where people act.

RB22 brings a new power unit, a changed aerodynamic framework and a renewed visual identity into one racing system. The Zebra opportunity is to make subsystem and operating boundaries equally clear: recognizable engagement states, disciplined marking zones, accessible service tasks and task-based energy validation. Launch renders explain presentation; photographs and attributed engineering sources ground the analysis without claiming that images prove performance.
