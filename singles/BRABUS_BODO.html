01 · Design Highlights

Make the identity distinctive without making every action unfamiliar.

BRABUS · One low envelope, a new identity
Coachbuilt proportion

One low envelope, a new identity

BRABUS wraps the Vanquish-based 2+2 in a carbon-fiber body. The long hood and low cabin preserve grand-touring proportion while the exterior establishes a separate identity. Top Gear reports retained donor multimedia and switchgear: the transformation changes the envelope more radically than the operating logic.

BRABUS · A grille that organizes the front
Functional signature

A grille that organizes the front

Thirteen vertical slats establish a repeated front signature. BRABUS describes two RAM-AIR ducts and separate cooling paths for radiators and front brakes. Design interpretation: use a consistent geometric rhythm to organize genuine functional openings rather than adding unrelated decorative vents.

BRABUS · The rear becomes a light signature
Night recognition

The rear becomes a light signature

The illuminated rear photograph reveals a continuous visual relationship between body width, lamp geometry and the central emblem. At night, the light pattern carries recognition with less dependence on paint or reflections. This is a visual reading; the image does not establish regulatory visibility or brightness.

BRABUS · Change the cabin, retain a familiar grammar
Operational continuity

Change the cabin, retain a familiar grammar

Carbon surfaces, a reshaped dashboard and bespoke leather surround a recognizable display-and-console layout. Top Gear identifies retained Vanquish multimedia and switchgear. For a highly customized product, familiarity in the controls can reduce the number of operating conventions that need to be relearned.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

A new body must still organize airflow, structure and service.

BRABUS · Performance starts with supporting systems
Powertrain packaging

Performance starts with supporting systems

BRABUS specifies a 5.2-litre biturbo V12, 735 kW and 1,200 Nm. The engine-bay image grounds the packaging discussion, while cooling and airflow remain system requirements beyond the engine itself. These figures are manufacturer claims; no acceleration or thermal performance is inferred from the photograph.

BRABUS · Airflow and exhaust share a compact boundary
Rear integration

Airflow and exhaust share a compact boundary

The rear combines a diffuser with vertically stacked, 3D-printed titanium exhaust outlets. BRABUS also describes an electrically deployed two-stage spoiler and an air-brake mode under specified conditions. The photograph shows the resting rear configuration, not evidence of spoiler deployment or measured downforce.

BRABUS · A wheel makes the process visible
Manufacturing geometry

A wheel makes the process visible

BRABUS describes forged 21-inch wheels with twenty spokes and CNC-machined concave geometry. The image makes the thin spoke rhythm and center detailing legible. The transferable principle is to let manufacturing constraints inform the visible structure; mass, stiffness and serviceability still require measured evaluation.

BRABUS · Turn the front into a legible system
Cooling architecture

Turn the front into a legible system

The straight-on view shows several intake zones rather than one undifferentiated opening. BRABUS assigns these paths to induction, radiators and brakes. Design interpretation: a functional face can communicate system hierarchy when the visible boundaries align with internal responsibilities.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

A nearly black palette gains depth through light, texture and repetition.

BRABUS · Repetition gives soft surfaces structure
Shell quilting

Repetition gives soft surfaces structure

The shell-like stitching turns a large leather field into a controlled pattern with visible depth and rhythm. BRABUS extends this motif to rear seating, the floor and luggage space. Design interpretation: repeated geometry can link separated components, but seam count and cleaning effort should remain part of the decision.

BRABUS · Glass interrupts the black volume
Roof-to-body contrast

Glass interrupts the black volume

The elevated coastal view exposes the relationship between the glass roof, long hood and body surfaces. BRABUS retains the fixed panoramic roof within the carbon body concept. A continuous dark palette still gains hierarchy from reflectance and transparency rather than relying on several accent colors.

BRABUS / via Car and Driver · Black surfaces need more than studio approval
Finish under real light

Black surfaces need more than studio approval

The exhibition photograph, published by Car and Driver and credited to BRABUS, places the dark body under event lighting. Highlights make surface transitions more visible than their nominal color suggests. Review a restrained CMF scheme across lighting conditions before using gloss as the main separator between parts.

BRABUS / via Car and Driver · Proportion changes in its operating context
Road-scale presence

Proportion changes in its operating context

The road photograph supplies environmental scale for the long front volume and compact cabin. Car and Driver describes a limited-speed drive, so the image supports presence and proportion rather than proof of claimed maximum performance. A product should be judged in use as well as against a controlled studio background.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Translate bespoke detailing into clearer controls, contact zones and service workflows.

BRABUS · Keep frequent actions physically distinct01

Keep frequent actions physically distinct

For handhelds and vehicle cradles, prototype differentiated physical controls for frequent actions. Check glove identification, reach and accidental activation before reducing controls to a uniform touch surface.

BRABUS · Assign materials to contact zones02

Assign materials to contact zones

Use material contrast to identify grip, support and display-facing zones on straps or mounts. Test friction, sweat, abrasion and cleaning; Nubuck is a reference for functional zoning, not a proposed enterprise material.

BRABUS · Make identity follow the assembly03

Make identity follow the assembly

Use one restrained surface or seam motif across terminal, boot and accessories. Align its boundaries with part breaks and repair interfaces, then review tolerance stacks so branding does not conceal assembly requirements.

BRABUS · Put service identity where work happens04

Put service identity where work happens

Explore a durable serial or QR marker at a service-access point, linking configuration, parts and repair history. BRABUS uses a luggage-compartment Spec Plate for its digital passport; Zebra’s workflow must remain useful even when network access is unavailable.

06 · Key Takeaway

The boldest transformation can preserve the most familiar actions.

BODO pairs a distinctive carbon body and bespoke tactile surfaces with established vehicle controls. For Zebra, the useful direction is selective transformation: make functional zones, accessories and service identity unmistakable while keeping frequent actions recognizable. Validate those changes with gloves, cleaning cycles and repair tasks before treating visual refinement as a usability improvement.
