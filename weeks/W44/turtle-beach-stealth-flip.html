Turtle Beach Stealth Flip Wireless Controller

A physical flip brings analog sticks and capacitive touchpads into one handheld control surface, with separate cursor input and configurable rear actions.

01 · Design Highlights

Switch input by moving the mechanism.

Flip without loose parts · turtlebeach.com
Rotating input

Flip without loose parts

The angled product view shows each thumb module turning within the controller rather than detaching. Keeping the stick and touch surface on opposite faces avoids a loose accessory during a mode change. The physical movement makes the available input mode visible before software changes are considered.

Same hand position · turtlebeach.com
Thumb reach

Same hand position

A three-quarter view places the sticks, face buttons and upper trackpad within one grip envelope. The central cursor surface adds PC navigation while the hands remain on the controller. Its actual reach and accidental-touch resistance still need evaluation with different hand sizes.

Two thumb surfaces · turtlebeach.com
Dual touch

Two thumb surfaces

The flipped presentation reveals a circular capacitive surface at each thumb position while retaining surrounding D-pad and face buttons. This turns the same handheld shell into an analog or touch-oriented interface. Users can select a control mode without storing a separate module.

Controls behind grip · turtlebeach.com
Rear inputs

Controls behind grip

The rear view exposes two programmable buttons under the fingers and trigger stops along the upper edge. Secondary actions migrate to a surface the thumbs do not occupy. Their utility depends on whether the buttons remain distinguishable during a firm, moving grip.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Keep sensing, profiles and connection legible.

Sense without contacts · turtlebeach.com
Magnetic sensing

Sense without contacts

The exploded stick image shows the thumb cap, shaft and sensing assembly rather than treating precision as a surface claim. Turtle Beach specifies Hall-effect sticks to resist drift. Magnetic measurement reduces dependence on a rubbing potentiometer, although durability of the surrounding mechanism still requires testing.

Short or full throw · turtlebeach.com
Trigger travel

Short or full throw

The top profile makes the shoulder controls and travel-stop area visible above the handles. A two-position stop changes trigger throw for quick response or longer modulation without another controller. The same gesture can support different tasks, provided the selected stop is legible by touch.

Carry the connection · turtlebeach.com
Receiver storage

Carry the connection

An underside perspective shows the rear housing and a small USB receiver associated with PC wireless play. Keeping the connection accessory with the hand tool reduces the chance of arriving without it. The design still needs a retention and loss test across repeated transport.

Settings beyond hardware · turtlebeach.com
Profile software

Settings beyond hardware

The PC Swarm II screen presents remapping, stick curves and profile controls beside the physical device. Four onboard profiles let settings travel with the controller after desktop configuration. This separates frequent in-hand actions from less frequent setup, but profile state must be easy to confirm.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

Shape makes the control mode readable.

Read two modes · turtlebeach.com
Surface coding

Read two modes

The low perspective reveals raised analog controls beside smooth circular touch areas and a sculpted upper cursor pad. Shape and texture distinguish three kinds of input on one black body. Users can locate the right surface by touch when the visual contrast is limited.

Grip mass and clearance · turtlebeach.com
Side contour

Grip mass and clearance

The shallow side view exposes the handle thickness, shoulder profile and protruding thumbsticks. The body must support a secure palm grip without putting the flipped modules in the way. Side clearance matters as much as the front graphic when a device moves between hands and storage.

Find the upper pad · turtlebeach.com
Cursor boundary

Find the upper pad

A close view frames the dedicated PC trackpad with a clear molded edge above the two thumb modules. The boundary differentiates cursor work from the rotating input surfaces below. This separation should reduce mode confusion, though it also adds another touch target to learn.

Mark active surfaces · turtlebeach.com
Mode affordance

Mark active surfaces

The angled feature image pairs a lit capacitive ring with raised buttons and a dark upper pad. A restrained outline calls attention to the active touch region without illuminating every control. The visual language can guide discovery if it remains readable in low light.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Test modes in the operator’s hands.

Turtle Beach · Separate input modes · product mechanism reference01

Separate input modes

The illustrated face shows stick and touch controls in one grip. Zebra could test a deliberate scan-first to cursor-first transition with tactile confirmation and a visible mode indicator, then measure accidental changes while walking.

Turtle Beach · Design for gloves · product mechanism reference02

Design for gloves

The close view distinguishes the smooth central trackpad from raised physical inputs. Zebra should compare touch navigation against a tactile scan trigger under gloves and wet hands, preserving activation when capacitive input becomes unreliable.

Turtle Beach · Map secondary actions · product mechanism reference03

Map secondary actions

The rear view identifies two mappable buttons beneath the fingers. Zebra could assign scan and confirm to distinct positions, then validate force, reach, state feedback and unintended activation during repeated pick-and-scan tasks.

Turtle Beach · Carry profiles into docks · product mechanism reference04

Carry profiles into docks

The PC Swarm II screen shows button mapping beside the controller. Zebra could preserve operator configurations across handheld and mounted workflows, while exposing the active profile and providing a simple recovery path after docking.

06 · Key Takeaway

A mode change should be felt and seen.

Stealth Flip keeps two input methods on one controller and gives each a physical state. For Zebra, the lesson is to use a deliberate, discoverable transition when a field device serves different tasks, then validate that state with gloves, motion, docks, and configurable profiles rather than treating touch as a universal replacement for tactile controls.
