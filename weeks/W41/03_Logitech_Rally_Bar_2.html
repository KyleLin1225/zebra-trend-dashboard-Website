Logitech Rally Bar 2

A low-profile meeting bar turns camera, audio, sensing and service access into one managed room component.

01 · Design Highlights

The bar keeps complex capture hardware visually simple at the front of the room.

Close-up of the camera lens on Logitech Rally Bar 2 in graphite.
Optical center

The lens becomes the visual anchor

The close-up gives the Rally Bar 2 camera a clear central position within a dark acoustic front. Logitech specifies a one-inch sensor and 115-degree field of view, letting one fixture cover a broad room while keeping the optical target easy to locate. The visual center also helps occupants understand where capture is directed.

Cutaway illustration of Logitech Rally Bar 2 in off-white showing the internal speakers, camera and microphone array.
Audio integration

A long face hides multiple systems

The cutaway shows drivers and microphone openings spread through one low horizontal enclosure. Logitech combines six beamforming microphones with four speakers so capture and playback occupy a single mounting zone rather than separate tabletop units. This can simplify room setup, although acoustic performance still depends on room size and reflective surfaces.

Modern conference room with Logitech Rally Bar 2 mounted below a large display and Logitech Tap IP 2 on the meeting table.
Room posture

The device occupies a familiar sightline

The installed room view places Rally Bar 2 below the main display and a touch controller on the table. Aligning camera and displayed participants makes remote eye contact easier to interpret than a camera placed off-axis, while the controller remains within seated reach. The arrangement separates capture from meeting control without scattering hardware.

Close-up of off-white Logitech Rally Bar 2 below a display, showing rear cable connections and mounting bracket.
Bracket reach

A mount can expose the rear

The off-white bar below the display reveals the support and cable area. Logitech says the wall mount pivots 90 degrees for service, turning a normally hidden rear face into an accessible maintenance surface. That reduces the need to remove a heavy installed unit just to inspect a port or cable.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Modular connections and accessible mounting make the installed system easier to service.

Close-up of the rear connection panel of Logitech Rally Bar 2 in graphite, showing a connected cable and additional ports.
Connection zone

Ports are grouped behind the body

The close-up of the rear shows cables entering a recessed connection area rather than breaking the front silhouette. Logitech Link and standard ports give installers paths for additional microphones and cameras while preserving the room-facing surface. A compact service zone can speed troubleshooting, but it must leave enough hand clearance.

Logitech Rally Bar 2 in off-white shown at an angle next to its mounting bracket below a display.
Single-person install

Mount geometry reduces labor

The angled off-white view shows the bar and bracket as separable installation parts. Logitech describes a setup optimized for one person, with the wall support placed before the full device is settled into position. This matters in distributed rooms where installers may have limited time and cannot rely on a second pair of hands.

Logitech Rally Bar 2 in off-white mounted below a display with the mounting and cable management area exposed.
Service swing

The cable side is intentionally reachable

The wall-mount image exposes the space behind the device as it pivots away from the display. That mechanical travel makes connectors and fastening points reachable while the unit remains attached, reducing disruption during a repair or configuration change. The clearance envelope is therefore a design requirement, not merely empty space.

Logitech Rally Bar 2 bulk device management shown in Logitech Sync on a desktop monitor.
Fleet view

Management extends beyond the room

The Logitech Sync screen represents Rally Bar 2 as a managed fleet device rather than an isolated camera. Remote configuration and health checks move routine service from a ladder in every room to a central dashboard, while physical access remains available for faults. The two service paths can reduce downtime across a large estate.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

Material, openings and status details communicate durable enterprise equipment.

Exploded view of Logitech Rally Bar 2 in graphite, showing internal speakers and components.
Layered enclosure

Internal architecture explains the shape

The exploded image separates a dark outer profile from speakers and other internal modules. Packing optical, acoustic and compute systems along one bar gives a visually tidy meeting room but requires organized thermal, service and cable paths behind it. The image is evidence of packaging logic, not proof of field durability.

Rally Bar 2 is intentionally designed with parts that can be easily separated and sorted by e-waste recyclers.
End-of-life access

Parts are designed to separate

The dismantled view shows the housing as separable material layers. Logitech says the parts are intended to be sorted by e-waste recyclers, making disassembly a planned end-of-life operation rather than destructive improvisation. A smooth exterior can therefore coexist with service and recovery access if fasteners and material breaks are considered early.

Logitech Rally Bar 2 with two participants, showing audio coverage during a video meeting on dual displays.
Voice field

Audio behavior is spatial

The diagram places participants at different positions relative to the mounted bar and illustrates sound coverage. Logitech's Right Sound processing aims to balance voices while suppressing noise and echo, so hardware distribution and software tuning share the task. The room benefits when distant speakers remain intelligible without adding visible microphones everywhere.

Close-up angled view of the Logitech Rally Bar 2 highlighting its aluminum unibody construction.
Aluminum shell

Material carries both form and function

The angled close-up highlights a continuous aluminum body around Rally Bar 2. Its restrained dark face puts the camera and acoustic surface ahead of decoration, while the metal housing gives the large fixture a coherent edge. The finish can support a durable impression, but touch points and scuffs still need validation in deployment.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Shared equipment needs clear sensor state and low-friction service after installation.

Pivot for service

Give vehicle-mounted terminals and fixed scanners a controlled service swing; measure cable access and tool clearance without removing the main bracket. Record maintenance time and reach from typical mounting heights.

Show camera state

Pair motorized shutters or covers with a visible state cue; verify that nearby workers can identify when capture is active at a glance. Check recognition from the doorway and meeting table.

Centralize fleet health

Expose temperature, connection and peripheral state to remote management while keeping local diagnostic access; test recovery after a failed update or disconnected cable. Measure recovery time across a representative site fleet.

Design disassembly early

Plan housing separation and material sorting before the aesthetic shell is fixed; verify that authorized service can reach wear parts without damaging the enclosure. Time component access and material sorting during teardown.

06 · Key Takeaway

Installation and service are part of the interface.

Rally Bar 2 joins a wide camera, microphones, speakers, sensors and management software behind a restrained body, while its mount exposes the rear for servicing. Zebra can apply the same thinking to shared kiosks, vehicle-mounted terminals and docked field systems.
