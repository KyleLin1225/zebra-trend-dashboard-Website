01 · Design Highlights

The kit scales beyond a single bar by placing each function where the room needs it.

Close-up cross-section of a video bar showing internal speaker drivers and directional beamlines representing audio projection.
Distributed kit

Each module has a clear job

The product composition separates camera, speakers, mic pods and RoomMate compute instead of hiding everything in one housing. Logitech can position each part for sightline or acoustic coverage while the system remains one managed installation. The approach offers more reach than a fixed bar, with the cost of more mounting decisions.

A multi-purpose training or conference room setup with rows of modular desks, chairs, dual front displays, and ceiling-mounted AV equipment.
Directed sound

Acoustic form follows the room

The cross-section graphic illustrates a speaker array sending controlled sound toward listeners. Logitech's Sound Shape approach uses multiple drivers and tuned beams to spread intelligible audio through large rooms rather than relying on a ceiling speaker grid. The benefit depends on positioning and room geometry, so setup is part of performance.

Four-panel layout displaying separate components of the Rally System 2 Pro, including a video speaker bar, PTZ camera, two mic pods, and the RoomMate 2 Pro hub unit.
Training room

The system spans many seats

The classroom image places capture and audio hardware across a deeper seating area than a compact meeting room. Distributing components lets the system address a presenter, distant participants and front displays without forcing one device to do every task. The room stays more usable when hardware placement follows the actual activity zones.

Logitech hardware system showcasing connected cameras, a video bar, a mic pod, and the central Logitech RoomMate 2 hub unit wired with category cables.
Modular map

The architecture is legible

The four-panel image separates Rally AI Camera Pro, speakers, microphones and RoomMate 2 Pro into distinct elements. That visual decomposition makes installation and replacement paths easier to understand than an anonymous network of boxes. Logitech Link then supplies a common connection method, reducing bespoke wiring as a room grows.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Standardized links and room scenes turn hardware changes into manageable configurations.

Dual setup of two complete Logitech video conferencing systems side by side, connected via central RoomMate 2 Pro hub units to support divided meeting spaces.
Cable topology

Standard cable carries expansion

The connected-system diagram shows cameras, mic pods and speakers leading toward the central compute unit. Logitech says its Link architecture uses category cabling for AV-over-IP expansion, letting installers extend coverage without a unique cable for every peripheral. A common topology makes future room changes more predictable and easier to document.

Image of Rally Speaker 2
Divisible rooms

One plan can become two

The dual-system image places two room installations side by side with their central hardware. Logitech supports scenes that split or combine a space, so the same physical room can expose different capture and audio zones without rewiring for each meeting. The design challenge moves from permanent partitioning to clear configuration state.

Image of Rally Speaker 2 Pro
Baseline speaker

Audio modules form a ladder

The Rally Speaker 2 image presents a compact rectangular unit as the base sound module in the family. Logitech describes a seven-driver linear array for coverage across an eight-meter space, allowing speaker capacity to match a room rather than defaulting to the largest model. That preserves system scale while limiting unnecessary hardware.

Image of Rally Speaker 2 Max
Pro speaker

More drivers, wider reach

Rally Speaker 2 Pro retains the same calm rectangular language while increasing the internal driver matrix. Logitech says its 32-driver Sound Shape system directs audio across roughly 14 meters, extending coverage for larger seats and reflective rooms. The consistent exterior supports a family identity, while the internal configuration changes the acoustic role.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

A distributed system should make its modules and active configuration inspectable.

Define modular roles

Separate sensing, display, audio and compute into serviceable site-station modules; verify each connection can be identified and replaced without rebuilding the full workstation. Time module replacement and verify labeling across two layouts.

Make scenes explicit

Show the active configuration when a warehouse station changes from packing to inspection; test whether workers can detect a wrong sensor or audio route. Measure correction time after deliberately switching the wrong scene.

Standardize expansion

Use common cable and mounting interfaces for optional peripherals; measure setup time and troubleshooting steps when a site adds a second camera or reader. Compare install steps across one- and two-camera configurations.

Expose availability

Place a glanceable status cue outside shared rooms or work cells; verify that color, text and position remain unambiguous across lighting and accessibility conditions. Measure false occupancy assumptions at several viewing distances.

06 · Key Takeaway

Modularity succeeds when configuration remains visible.

Rally System 2 Pro combines a separate camera, speakers, mic pods and RoomMate compute, with standard cabling and software scenes for divided rooms. Zebra can use the same pattern for sites and vehicles where sensing, display and input modules must change without redesigning the entire station.
