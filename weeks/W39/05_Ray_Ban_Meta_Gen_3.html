05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Test the physical and social consequences of head-worn capture before transferring the form to work.

01 · Tactile input

Prototype a distinguishable shortcut for scanning or AI assistance; verify thumb reach and glove recognition before shrinking the button.

02 · Long-shift ergonomics

Measure temple and nose contact across face sizes, movement and safety eyewear requirements instead of treating mass alone as comfort.

03 · State visibility

Show capture and AI-listening states clearly to the operator and nearby people; test whether the feedback is understandable without an app.

04 · Shared architecture

Use one validated compute and audio platform across fit variants, while making the replaceable wear-contact parts and service path explicit.

06 · Key Takeaway

A discreet wearable still needs an unmistakable operating state.

Gen 3 improves styling choice and fit while adding a customizable AI button, six microphones and camera capture. For Zebra, the transferable design work is to pair long-wear comfort with tactile certainty and clear sensing feedback in shared operational spaces.
