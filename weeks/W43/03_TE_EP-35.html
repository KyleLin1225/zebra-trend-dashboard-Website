01 · Design Highlights

A sampler's visual language becomes a handle you operate.

EP-133 K.O. II · teenage.engineering
K.O. FAMILY GRILLE LANGUAGE

One grille language, two machines

EP-133 K.O. II set teenage engineering's K.O. vocabulary: a perforated grille, exposed fastener dots, a flat grey slab. EP-35 repeats that grille pitch and fastener rhythm at amplifier scale, so the family reads as one system through surfacing logic, not a shared logo or color block.

Handle control deck · teenage.engineering
HANDLE-AS-CONTROL-SURFACE

The handle is the dashboard

Lift the handle and the interface is already in your hand: line input, guitar jack, phantom-powered XLR, USB-C and the EP-Plug port all ring the grip. Carrying position and operating position collapse into one posture, so setup starts the moment the case leaves the shelf.

Storage hatch, light on · teenage.engineering
STORAGE-HATCH EASTER EGG

A hatch that lights itself

Behind the rear grille sits a cable bay that switches on a built-in camp light the instant the hatch swings down. It rewards curiosity rather than announcing itself on a spec sheet, turning dead storage space into a flashlight nobody had to charge separately.

K.O. Dock Kit clips · teenage.engineering
MODULAR EP-GRID STACKING

Clips turn the case into a rack

The K.O. Dock Kit's four orange clips bolt straight into the grille's mounting holes, letting a second EP-series device — sampler, mixer or pedal — ride on the amp's own housing. Stacking becomes a hardware feature built into the chassis, not an accessory improvised after the fact.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Inputs, pairing and presets share one simple logic.

EP-2350 FX-Mic · teenage.engineering
DUAL SINGLE-KNOB EFFECTS LOGIC

One knob swaps the whole effect

Guitar and vocal signals each get eight dedicated presets, chosen with one knob instead of a menu of individual parameters. The companion EP-2350 FX-Mic repeats that same single-knob gesture, so a vocalist reaches for an identical control whether the effect lives in the amp or the microphone.

Paired stereo units · teenage.engineering
AURACAST TILT-TO-PAIR MECHANISM

Tilt the case to pick a channel

Pairing two K.O.-AMPs over Bluetooth Auracast is a physical gesture, not a settings menu: tilting one unit left or right assigns it to that stereo channel. Orientation sensing replaces an on-screen toggle with a motion anyone can read at a glance, including mid-set with both hands already full.

Handy Mic accessory · teenage.engineering
PHANTOM-POWERED XLR INPUT

A studio mic input, field-portable

The rear grille hides an XLR input with switchable 48V phantom power, the same provision a studio interface gives a condenser microphone. The companion Handy Mic plugs straight in, its push-to-talk button standing in for a mixer channel's mute switch — one cable replacing a small vocal signal chain.

Amptenna accessory · teenage.engineering
EP-PLUG MODULAR PORT

One port, several swappable roles

The handle's EP-Plug port is a single mechanical and electrical socket, not a dedicated antenna mount. Plugging in the Amptenna turns the amp into an FM tuner instead, proof that one small connector can carry more than one accessory role as teenage engineering's EP-Plug lineup grows.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

Color and proportion carry more meaning than decoration.

Add-On Bundle packaging · teenage.engineering
SURFACING & PARTING-LINE LANGUAGE

Molding lines drawn as the design

The bundle box's own isometric line drawing doubles as a surfacing diagram: parting lines, rib structure and port cutouts appear as the product's actual visual language, not smoothed away under one shell. Showing the molding logic this openly treats manufacturing geometry as a legitimate finish rather than something to disguise.

Eyelet Bag hardware · teenage.engineering
ORANGE COLOR-AS-FUNCTION CODING

One hue migrates off the chassis

The record button and effects-preset rings on the amp itself use the same saturated orange as the Eyelet Bag's round corner grommets and webbing trim. A single hue carried from hard switch to soft good turns color into a cross-product signal for 'interactive' or 'EP-series,' rather than a one-off choice.

K.O. combo pairing · teenage.engineering
HANDLE-TO-GRILLE PROPORTION

A raised band shows where to lift

Set beside the flat-deck EP-133, the K.O.-AMP's silhouette reads almost entirely through its handle band and grille block: a shallow top strip over a deep lower mass. That two-thirds-to-one-third split signals carry point and sound-output zone before a single input or dial gets read up close.

Pocket Bag fit · teenage.engineering
ACCESSORY FAMILY VISUAL CONSISTENCY

Soft goods repeat the hardware's rules

The Pocket Bag's woven 'EP SERIES' strap crosses the case at the same height as the handle band it covers, and the grille's texture reads faintly through the bag's ribbed weave. Protective accessories quote the hardware's proportion and texture at a smaller scale, not just its fit.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Translate K.O.-AMP's physical logic into field hardware.

EP-127 K.O. Pedal · teenage.engineering01

Give feet a dual-zone switch

The K.O. Pedal's two pressure-sensitive pads assign separate functions to left and right foot pressure. Zebra could prototype a glove-operable dual-zone control for hands-busy scanning, then test whether both zones stay distinguishable through gloves.

Preloaded USB drive · teenage.engineering02

Make storage a usable bonus

EP-35 hides a camp light and a two-sided sample drive behind one hatch, turning spare storage into a used feature. Zebra could reserve one door for a configuration drive, testing whether field staff open it.

Textile guitar cable · teenage.engineering03

Color-code cables by function

EP-35's woven guitar cable looks and feels distinct from a standard line cable at a glance. Zebra could extend sheath color and texture coding across peripheral cables, testing recognition speed for technicians in low light.

Carry strap · teenage.engineering04

Make the carry point removable

EP-35's strap clips on only when needed, instead of living permanently on the chassis. Zebra could offer a detachable strap or handle mount for handhelds, then test one-handed draw speed without it.

06 · Key Takeaway

A handle isn't just a grip — it's the control panel.

EP-35 folds line, guitar and phantom-powered XLR inputs, Bluetooth pairing and effects control into the same handle a user already grips to carry the unit. For Zebra, the transferable move isn't the amp's styling — it's treating the carry point as real interface real estate, then verifying operability with gloves, wet hands and one-handed carry before adding it anywhere.
