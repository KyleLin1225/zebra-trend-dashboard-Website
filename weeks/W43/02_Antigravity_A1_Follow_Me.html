Antigravity A1 Follow Me Bundle

Antigravity swaps the A1's goggles and joystick for a 44.7g touchscreen watch and an 8.2g single-button ring, wagering that gesture control plus GPS-backed tracking can out-simplify a traditional drone controller — while quietly keeping a hardware button in reserve.

01 · Design Highlights

Two Wearables Replace the Goggles-and-Joystick Kit

Mat Smith for Engadget
01 · Wrist Hardware

A Wrist Screen Replaces Goggles

The 44.7g Watch Remote packs a 1.72-inch touchscreen, IP67 sealing and GNSS into a single strap, replacing the A1's goggles and joystick kit entirely. A physical button still sits beside the screen for takeoff and record, so a misread tap or wet glass never strands the pilot mid-flight.

Mat Smith for Engadget
02 · Finger Hardware

One Button, No Screen

At 8.2g, the Ring Remote is the whole kit's lightest component: no screen, no mode dial, just one button that must be held to arm Drag-to-Fly. That minimalism keeps the finger-worn piece unobtrusive, but it also means every gesture command rides on a single physical input.

Mat Smith for Engadget
03 · Protective Shell

Guards That Can't Fold Away

Full-coverage propeller guards enclose all four rotors on every side, and Engadget found they also block the A1's normal fold-flat arms, so the drone stays bulky in a bag whenever guards are on. Antigravity still recommends outdoor flying only, with at least two meters of vertical clearance at launch.

Antigravity official image, via Newsshooter
04 · Single-Limb System

Both Controls Share One Arm

The Watch and Ring Remotes are built to live on the same wrist and hand, a deliberate break from the A1's two-handed goggles-and-joystick setup. Wearing both on one limb frees the other hand for a handlebar, a ski pole or a camera — the bundle's real pitch to solo athletes.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Sensor Fusion and Modular Parts Define the Upgrade

Antigravity official image, via Imaging Resource
01 · Sensor Fusion

GPS Backs Up the Camera

Deep Track's camera-only tracking is the A1's known weak point, reportedly struggling with fast subjects like cyclists or skiers. The Watch Remote adds a GNSS receiver, fusing absolute position with visual recognition so the drone keeps its lock even when the subject briefly ducks behind an obstacle.

Antigravity official image, via Geeky Gadgets
02 · Subject Selection

Tap the Subject, Not a Stick

Before Deep Track can follow anyone, the pilot taps a bounding box around the subject on the Watch Remote's small touchscreen. That single tap replaces joystick-driven subject acquisition from the standard kit, which speeds up setup but asks a 1.72-inch screen to register an accurate selection in direct sunlight.

Antigravity official image, via LensVid
03 · Modular Accessories

A Kit Built From Swappable Parts

The Follow Me Bundle packages the drone, guards, battery, Watch Remote, Ring Remote, charging grip and case as separable modules, not one fused unit. Existing A1 owners can buy just the $339.99 accessory kit instead of a new drone, turning what looks like a product launch into a parts-level upgrade.

Mihail Svilenov, via DroneXL
04 · Weight Budget

Protection Costs Flight Time

The full-coverage cage adds enough mass to push the A1's takeoff weight from 249g to roughly 351g with the larger battery, crossing into a stricter drone weight class in several regions. Reviewers also clocked flight time dropping from about 39 minutes bare to roughly 23 minutes once guards are mounted.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

Function Dictates Color, Shape and Material Here

Antigravity official image, via Newsshooter
01 · Screen Hierarchy

One Glance, One Color Cue

On the Watch Remote's 1.72-inch face, a red Stop button sits against a live video thumbnail and status icons, giving the pilot one unmistakable color cue at a glance. That hierarchy holds up standing still, but a wrist screen this small still asks a lot of legibility mid-activity.

Antigravity official image, via Newsshooter
02 · Repeating Structure

Four Identical Rings, One Logic

Each of the four propeller guards is an identical radial spoke pattern, not a styled shroud — the same cage shape repeated four times signals a mass-manufactured safety part rather than a bespoke enclosure. That honesty about function costs the A1 its sleek silhouette once all four click into place.

Jake Sloan, via DroneXL
03 · Cold-Weather Input

One Button, Even With Gloves

A hands-on test in the Alaska mountains put the Ring Remote's single button under a thick glove — the hardest case for a screen-free design. A button large and firm enough to find blind is the only thing standing between a gloved hand and a lost gesture.

Antigravity official image, via Newsshooter
04 · Status Color Coding

A Blue LED Does the Talking

The Ring Remote's charging puck uses one blue LED against a white shell and black strap, the kit's only color-as-status signal outside the watch screen. It is a small, legible gesture in an otherwise all-white-and-black accessory family, where material changes, not printed icons, mark where the electronics actually live.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Four Sketchable Moves From Wrist and Ring Controls

01

Hardware Trigger as Fallback

Keep a hardware trigger as a gesture-independent fallback on wrist- and ring-worn scanners, verified under gloves, in rain and in low light, so a missed tap or an unreadable screen never strands a worker mid-scan.

02

Fuse GNSS With Vision

Pair GNSS with visual tracking so a handheld scanner keeps its lock on fast-moving totes at conveyor speed, then re-verify outdoors near metal racking, where GPS multipath most often breaks a single-sensor approach.

03

Tool-Free Snap-On Bumper

Design a tool-free, quick-attach bumper that fully encloses the camera and scan window, not just the corners, then drop-test the device both attached and removed so the guard never becomes mandatory for its base rating.

04

Protection as a Swappable Layer

Treat impact protection as a swappable layer selected per task risk, not a fixed-weight permanent shell: a light courier device skips the bumper, a dock-heavy warehouse unit wears it, and nobody carries unneeded armor.

06 · Key Takeaway

A single button only works if a hardware fallback survives it.

Antigravity's wrist-and-ring system proves gesture control can replace a two-handed controller, but its own reviewers still leaned on the Watch Remote's physical button when touch or voice failed. For Zebra, the lesson isn't to chase gesture control for its own sake — it's to keep one hardware escape hatch beside any new touch or voice interface, tested in gloves, rain and glare before it ships.
