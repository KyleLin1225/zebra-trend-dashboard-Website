Keep frequent actions outside the menu

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Separate interfaces that change from infrastructure that must endure.

Panasonic Connect US · product visualization · Concentrate change in defined modules
Replaceable interfaces

Concentrate change in defined modules

The US exploded xPAK image shows expansion modules separately from the tablet body. Panasonic identifies four modular areas: battery, SSD and two expansion bays. Zebra can isolate changing scan, radio or accessory interfaces behind explicit service boundaries, with access tools, seal restoration and configuration identification defined before claiming that modularity reduces downtime.

Show how options relate to the chassis

The official rear view locates two open expansion areas within the tablet shell. Panasonic lists optional barcode, serial and credential functions; the photo does not establish every configuration. A Zebra configuration map should state compatible combinations, seal recovery and service ownership rather than presenting modules without their mechanical and power constraints.

Protect ports without hiding their purpose

The official side view exposes several ports behind individual protective covers. Panasonic specifies Thunderbolt, USB and keyboard-side legacy connections, but the image alone cannot establish electrical compatibility. Zebra can differentiate frequent connections from protected service ports and test cap retention, access and strain relief through the complete task sequence.

Battery exchange is a workflow state

The official underside image shows the two battery-bay locations rather than a cell or thermal test. Panasonic specifies hot swap only when an optional second battery is installed. 05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Separate durable scan-device structure from changing radio, battery and interface modules; make gloved controls and dock engagement states tangible.

01

Make glove actions tangible

Prototype scan and recovery controls with distinct location, travel and confirmation. Test representative gloves alongside touchscreen targets, including wet conditions and accidental touches. Keep the primary action reachable while secondary settings change.

02

Design the complete dock transition

Model carry, partial engagement, locking, charging and release as separate states. Verify both physical retention and application continuity with cables and accessories attached, and provide an unmistakable indication when the device is ready to work.

03

Define replaceable boundaries early

Separate battery, interface and data-storage service areas from the core structure. Specify access tools, seals, identification and allowed combinations, then measure replacement effort and fault isolation rather than equating options with easy maintenance.

04

Preserve fleet investment deliberately

Map legacy docks and peripherals by exact configuration. State limits clearly, test strain relief and connector access, and retain a stable interaction grammar across generations so hardware upgrades do not force operators to relearn frequent tasks.

06 · Key Takeaway

Build continuity into the interfaces that people touch and fleets reuse.

TOUGHBOOK 34’s most transferable relationship is between operator continuity and hardware change: physical controls remain locatable, the keyboard changes posture, and selected modules can evolve without replacing the entire workstation. For Zebra, evaluate dock compatibility, service boundaries and glove interaction together. The useful outcome is a task that survives a hardware transition, not an assumption that adding more options automatically improves field reliability.
