01 · Design Highlights

A smoother front changes both appearance and the thumb's resting behavior.

A compact body fits a travel use case

The Verge's hands-on photo shows the six-inch reader's small overall footprint; its flush face replaces the former raised-bezel look. Amazon describes a flush-front display across the redesigned family. Removing the raised bezel makes the face easier to wipe and gives a continuous reading plane, but it also leaves less physical separation between a resting thumb and a touch-sensitive page area.

Ube color

Color reaches the edge without invading text

The Ube shell frames a monochrome reading field, making the housing expressive while the content remains neutral. The colored housing sits outside the grayscale content region, so product identity comes from the object rather than a permanent tint over the page. This preserves text contrast while differentiating variants; glare and perceived edge thickness still need assessment in actual light.

The side power control is visible near the upper-right edge.
Power-button edge

Power moves away from the hand support

The 2026 power key shifts to the upper-right edge, a direct response to unintended presses at the bottom of earlier Kindles. Amazon moved the power control to the upper-right side in response to accidental bottom-edge presses. The new position changes the contact sequence for a one-handed reader: the support hand can hold the lower edge without sharing space with an on/off command.

Two colored Kindle backs show the finish and material choices.
Aluminum edition

Material identifies a premium variant

The aluminum-bodied option changes perceived stiffness and finish while keeping the same flush-front control language. A metal-backed variant changes tactile temperature, stiffness and perceived durability without changing the display interaction. The material choice may appeal to a different buyer while preserving common software behavior; weight and grip under extended holding should be judged separately.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

The Kindle redesign gives Zebra direct hypotheses for grip zones, button placement and accessory controls.

Move vulnerable controls

Test power and trigger keys against common one-handed grips before freezing placement. Use actual gloves, wrist straps and cases in the mockup; record false presses during carrying, scanning and setting the device into its charger..

Add a safe resting zone

Explore touch regions that distinguish a supporting thumb from an intentional tap. Test the resting zone across hand sizes and orientations, then provide a visible recovery path when palm rejection suppresses a deliberate command..

Keep tactile input modular

Offer clip-on or dock-based physical controls for workflows that need them. Define pairing, attachment and battery state in the same interaction map, and verify that the default device remains usable when the accessory is absent..

Map materials to use

Use finish and color to identify role or durability tier without weakening screen legibility. Compare recognition under warehouse lighting, cleaning wear and fleet mixing, while keeping critical status readable without depending on housing color..

06 · Key Takeaway

The default surface stays simple because special controls move into deliberate accessories.

Amazon pairs a flush front with grip-aware touch behavior and optional physical page-turn inputs. For Zebra, the transferable move is to design the core handheld for broad use while providing tactile controls where a specific workflow or mount actually needs them.
