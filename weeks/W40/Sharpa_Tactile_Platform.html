01 · Design Highlights

Design contact as a continuous interaction, not a binary event.

Sharpa · W02 manufacturer render / illustration · Design for objects that already exist
Tool scale

Design for objects that already exist

The W02 manufacturer render shows fine tools held within a human-scale hand envelope. Sharpa specifies 21 active degrees of freedom and a compact structure. For Zebra, define the tool, grip and surrounding workspace first: a wearable scanner or accessory should fit existing hand motions rather than making operators reorganize their environment around new hardware.

Sharpa · W02 manufacturer render / illustration · Reduce the envelope with a clear reference
Generation change

Reduce the envelope with a clear reference

The labelled W01/W02 comparison makes the new hand’s smaller dimensions explicit and preserves the predecessor as a reference. The launch describes W02 as approximately 30% smaller. Zebra can similarly compare reach and clearance across device generations, but dimensions alone cannot establish comfort; test wrist loading, glove fit and nearby equipment in repeated tasks.

Sharpa · AE01 manufacturer render / illustration · Let the data interface follow the hand
Operator fit

Let the data interface follow the hand

The AE01 manufacturer render places the exoskeleton and retention system on a real-hand silhouette. Its product page lists four size options and 22 encoders. For Zebra wearables, separate fit adjustment from sensing alignment, then assess donning, removal and contact pressure across users instead of assuming that a human-looking mechanism accommodates every hand.

Sharpa · D01 manufacturer render / illustration · A task changes the complete envelope
Whole-body reach

A task changes the complete envelope

The D01 precision pose illustrates both hands reaching a shared work area; it is a render, not a measured assembly result. D01 combines 7-DoF arms and human-scale spherical wrists. For Zebra, evaluate body, cable and accessory clearance throughout a task sequence, including what happens when a user changes posture or reaches around an obstruction.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Match sensing resolution and response to the contact zone.

Sharpa · D01 manufacturer render / illustration · Sense contact beyond the fingertips
Body contact

Sense contact beyond the fingertips

The D01 e-skin illustration visualizes contact over a body surface. The product page specifies 100 Hz sampling and 0.2 N force resolution for body e-skin. Zebra can examine contact sensing for docks or grips, with clear thresholds and fault states; these figures do not establish stopping distance, collision safety or certification of a complete robot.

Sharpa · W02 manufacturer render / illustration · Use resolution where contact matters
Sensing zones

Use resolution where contact matters

The W02 graphic distinguishes fingertip tactile arrays from electronic skin over the rest of the hand. Manufacturer specifications give 1 mm fingertip spatial resolution and 5 mm for hand e-skin. Zebra can similarly match sensor density to the task zone, then explain uncertainty and contamination effects rather than treating every measured contact as equally informative.

Sharpa · AE01 manufacturer render / illustration · Haptics should describe an actionable state
Returned feedback

Haptics should describe an actionable state

The AE01 illustration highlights fingertip feedback rather than only motion capture. Sharpa specifies five LRA vibration motors with 256 dynamic levels per fingertip. For Zebra, choose patterns that distinguish success, partial engagement and recovery, and validate recognition under workload; a larger number of intensity levels is not proof that users can reliably interpret them.

Hesai · D01 supplier photograph · Coordinate perception with contact
Sensor integration

Coordinate perception with contact

Hesai’s supplier image shows the D01 chest-mounted optical sensor interface; its accompanying account links spatial perception to manipulation. This visible assembly does not reveal internal sensing performance. For Zebra, combine optical and physical-state information around a clear confidence model, so a scan or dock action can recover when one modality is occluded or ambiguous.

Let body, joint and feedback surfaces reveal different roles.

Expose articulation with a calm surface

The D01 shoulder detail differentiates a rounded joint from the smooth shell through a continuous seam. This manufacturer render supports an architectural reading, not a claim of low friction or impact protection. Zebra can use similar transitions to communicate adjustment or movement while independently validating pinch clearance, sealing and repair access.

Sharpa · D01 manufacturer render / illustration · Show where structure becomes dexterity

Show where structure becomes dexterity

The forearm-to-hand render transitions from a broad protective volume to smaller articulated elements. The visible contrast makes the hand’s working role readable. Zebra can apply that hierarchy to rugged housings and adjustable attachments, retaining clear service boundaries and avoiding decorative seams that imply a user-adjustable part when the mechanism is fixed.

The torso detail separates a smooth upper body, dark waist region and open lower support. This is a form interpretation of the D01 render. Zebra can distinguish protected electronics, flexible interfaces and mounting zones with restrained material boundaries, ensuring the visual hierarchy still tells an operator where to grip, attach and inspect.

Sharpa · AE01 manufacturer render / illustration · Make the human–machine relationship visible

Make the human–machine relationship visible

The paired robotic-hand and glove graphic uses corresponding poses to explain teleoperation. Sharpa describes mapping encoder data to the robot, with haptic feedback returning to the operator. Zebra can make a wearable-to-device relationship similarly explicit through setup and status cues, then test whether users understand which motion or event caused the feedback.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Explore task-specific haptic confirmation for scanning and docking; capture failed grasps and corrective actions as paired interaction data, with safe recovery and sensor uncertainty explicitly represented.

01

Make feedback mean a task state

Define distinct tactile cues for scan success, uncertainty and retry. Test recognition with gloves, motion and competing alerts. Provide a clear recovery action instead of using the same vibration for every event.

02

Sense engagement at the useful boundary

Explore force or contact sensing at a dock latch, grip or accessory interface. Pair it with mechanical state and sensor confidence; validate contamination, drift and interrupted power before using contact data to authorize operation.

03

Capture corrections as design evidence

Record attempted actions, failures and the operator’s correction together during prototypes. Use that sequence to improve feedback timing and hardware fit, with explicit consent and controls for any person-related data collected.

04

Prototype the complete wearable loop

Evaluate fit, donning, sensing alignment and feedback as one system. Include user variation, representative gloves and repeated shift tasks. Preserve a simple manual fallback whenever sensing or wireless communication becomes unreliable.

06 · Key Takeaway

A useful feedback loop makes contact, uncertainty and correction legible.

Sharpa’s integrated stack connects a contact-capable body, a tactile hand and an operator interface. The transferable idea is the loop between a sensed event and a meaningful correction. Zebra can apply that logic to grip, docking and scan confirmation, using feedback that explains the task state rather than merely vibrating. These launch capabilities remain manufacturer claims; contact sensing alone does not establish collaborative safety or autonomous reliability.
