05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Prototype quickly adjustable scanner straps and wearable retention as complete load paths; distinguish friction, impact and fit zones and test them under repeated movement.

01

Prototype adjustment with the whole load path

Evaluate dial, buckle and strap options with glove access, tension distribution and rapid release. Include repeated walking and arm motion so adjustment is judged by retained fit, not by how precise the mechanism feels on a table.

02

Separate friction and impact zones

Map where a scanner grip or wearable cradle transmits force and where it receives impact. Tune texture, compliance and protective material separately, then test cleaning, moisture and wear across the complete assembly.

03

Make correct fit visible and tangible

Provide recognizable loose, fitted and released states. Test whether operators can adjust equipment without removing gloves and whether a second user can identify incorrect fit before beginning work.

04

Design maintenance into the retention system

Keep wear surfaces inspectable and replacement steps clear. Assess trapped debris, strap cleaning and fastener access early, and avoid an integrated appearance that makes a damaged retention component require replacing the entire product.

06 · Key Takeaway

Design the contact system before decorating the protective shell.

FREERIDER’s useful relationship is between how the product is retained, where it transmits force and where it must absorb impact. The flat and clipless variants change the interface instead of merely changing a colorway, while closure and protection remain coordinated. Zebra can translate this into adjustable wearables and accessories with clear load paths, inspectable wear areas and feedback for correct fit. Field tests must establish any improvement in comfort or retention.
