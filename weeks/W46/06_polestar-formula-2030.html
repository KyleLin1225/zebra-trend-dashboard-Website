Polestar Formula 2030

A future-facing cockpit gives physical controls, adaptable information and material honesty distinct roles in one legible driving experience.

Formula 2030 cockpit and seats · Polestar
01 · Design Highlights

Physical affordances remain central to a configurable cockpit.

Console and physical control bank · Polestar
Control architecture

The console keeps controls in reach

The slim center console places physical controls directly below the portrait screen. Their location gives the driver an immediate tactile target while reserving the display for richer context. Polestar says the eight physical controls can be exchanged and reassigned, so configuration remains anchored in a graspable object.

Center display with separated functions · Polestar
Information density

A screen can carry layered tasks

The center display shows distinct information zones rather than one uninterrupted graphic. Climate, drive data and assistant functions occupy recognizable regions, supporting glanceable changes in attention. For a real driver, the spacing and hierarchy would need validation during movement rather than in a static render.

Steering controls and curved display · Polestar
Steering interface

Hands keep a direct control surface

The steering-wheel close-up puts button clusters at the driver grip and a curved information strip above them. This division supports frequent actions without forcing a reach to the center screen. The visible yellow detail is an orientation cue, while button identification still needs tactile and motion testing.

Seat latch and perforated surface · Polestar
Service touchpoint

A seat latch stays identifiable

The seat detail exposes a small pull point and surrounding perforated surface. A visible mechanical cue makes the adjustment or access action easier to locate than a hidden touch target. In a concept, this is a useful form-language direction; durability and service access require field validation.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Mode changes coordinate hardware, displays and vehicle feel.

Center tunnel mode selector · Polestar
Drive modes

The tunnel concentrates mode choice

The center tunnel shows a deliberate control cluster beside the screen, with the mode shifter distinguished from adjacent functions. Polestar connects that selection to screen, seat, steering-position and steering-feel changes. One action therefore needs an explicit transition and an unmistakable final state.

Curved driver information display · Polestar
Driver information

Cards let the display change focus

The curved driver display isolates speed and driving metrics in a narrow field near the sightline. Polestar describes switchable Calm, Map and Car-centric cards, making information priority a mode decision. Each card should preserve critical status and explain what information has moved elsewhere.

Open rear bay and underlying structure · Polestar
Package layout

Open space exposes a functional platform

The open rear bay reveals the vehicle floor and structural packaging rather than hiding every working volume. It makes the relationship between load access and the underlying architecture observable. The image supports a packaging lesson, although it does not establish production battery or cargo specifications.

Doors and hatch open · Polestar
Transformation

Access changes the vehicle silhouette

With doors and rear hatch open, Formula 2030 becomes a diagram of entry, loading and the compact four-door package. The wide openings dramatize use sequences as much as exterior form. Their sweep and reach would need clearance testing before any analogous field product adopts such a mechanism.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

Tonal restraint makes structural and material decisions visible.

Rear road view · Polestar
Exterior identity

The rear view stays visually quiet

The road photograph shows a broad rear stance and a restrained light signature without a large applied grille or ornament. Its limited graphic elements make the vehicle recognizable by proportion and lighting. That identity must stay clear across distance, weather and different operating states.

Rear light-blade graphic · Polestar
Light graphic

One rear blade defines the edge

The direct rear view isolates the horizontal light blade and the width of the tail. A single, continuous cue provides a stable recognition point against a minimal body surface. Polestar calls this the Single Blade expression, but visibility and repairability remain practical questions beyond the concept.

Exposed seat frame and headrest · Polestar
Material honesty

The seat frame remains legible

The isolated seat structure shows an exposed frame and a visibly distinct upholstered headrest. Polestar says low-emission galvanized steel is used for seat frames and the cross-car beam. Showing the structure makes the material story tangible while still demanding evidence of real sourcing and maintainability.

Woven seat material and accent · Polestar
Textile zoning

A quiet weave carries the accent

The close-up concentrates on a pale woven surface with a measured yellow accent. Polestar describes flax and bio-based polypropylene for the seat hardback and a grid-weave expression. The image illustrates how material identity can mark a functional zone without relying on a bright color field.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Make mode, control and service state tangible in Zebra hardware.

Physical controls at the steering wheel · Polestar01

Keep a tactile action core

The steering detail locates repeated actions within hand reach. For a Zebra vehicle terminal or wearable controller, prototype a small set of glove-readable physical inputs; test errors during vibration and shift changes.

Mode selector and adjacent knobs · Polestar02

Expose a mode transition

The control close-up separates the yellow selector from neighboring knobs. For Zebra docks and vehicle-mounted computers, make Normal, scan-intensive and service modes explicit through one deliberate control, a visible transition and confirmation on the display.

Seat access, harness and contact zone · Polestar03

Design for access and maintenance

The open cabin shows where occupants, hardware and service surfaces meet. For a Zebra fixed station, map the reach path to power, consumables and mounts; verify maintenance with gloves in a crowded installation.

Side profile with open doors · Polestar04

Make the form show state

The side-open view exposes the complete access sequence and clearance envelope. Zebra vehicle accessories can distinguish deployed, stowed and service states with physical geometry, then test operator clearance in the target vehicle.

06 · Key Takeaway

Adaptability works when every change stays physically understandable.

Formula 2030 does not treat a larger screen as the answer to every task. Its configurable physical controls, card-based driver information and coordinated mode changes suggest a stronger rule for Zebra: keep frequent actions tactile, show state changes on the device and make service access legible in the hardware. Prototype the interaction with gloves, motion and real maintenance tasks before adopting the concept language.
