01 · Design Highlights

Clear viewing begins with stable contact

Nose contact area · www.xreal.com
Nose contact area

Spread load without losing alignment

XREAL describes a broader nose-pad contact area intended to improve stability and comfort. Spreading the support region changes how the frame rests on the face while helping maintain the optical viewing position. The relationship is between local pressure and repeatable screen alignment, rather than softness considered independently from the viewing experience.

Temple adjustment · www.xreal.com
Temple adjustment

Make fit an adjustable relationship

The official fit images show adjustable temples alongside the revised nose support. Changing the frame angle lets the wearer position the optical system relative to the eyes without redesigning the display itself. This makes initial setup consequential: adjustment needs to be understandable, repeatable and resistant to movement if the screen is to remain consistently usable.

IPD size options · www.xreal.com
IPD size options

Treat optical fit as product architecture

XREAL offers two interpupillary-distance size options rather than promising one universal optical fit. Size selection works alongside the physical adjustment system to place the viewing region around the user's eyes. The implication is that product sizing and setup instructions participate in image quality; a nominally excellent display can still disappoint when the optical geometry is mismatched.

Portable workspace · www.xreal.com
Portable workspace

Move the display beyond the desk

The workspace image presents the glasses as a display connected to an existing computing setup. Moving the viewing surface away from a physical monitor changes where information can appear while leaving input devices and the host computer in the workflow. The experience depends on readable content and stable placement, not merely the perceived size of the projected screen.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Distribute capability around the wearer

Native spatial processing · www.xreal.com
Native spatial processing

Keep spatial processing on the glasses

XREAL identifies its X1 chip as the basis for native three-degree-of-freedom spatial viewing. Processing head orientation within the glasses supports a screen that can stay spatially placed rather than simply moving with every head turn. This architecture reduces reliance on an additional processing accessory for the core experience, while translation tracking remains a separate capability requiring careful product-specific qualification.

Host-device compatibility · www.xreal.com
Host-device compatibility

Account for the complete connection path

The compatibility presentation places the glasses alongside several existing entertainment and computing devices. The experience depends on a supported host connection, so portability includes the cable and any necessary interface hardware rather than the glasses alone. Design evaluation should follow the setup sequence: what users carry, connect, configure and recover when a host does not provide the expected display output.

Eye-comfort positioning · www.xreal.com
Eye-comfort positioning

Separate comfort claims from task evidence

XREAL presents eye-comfort certification as one part of the product's viewing proposition. The official illustration frames prolonged screen use as an experience that depends on display behavior as well as wearing geometry. Certification is useful context, but it does not establish comfort for an industrial task; text legibility, workload and uninterrupted wearing duration still need direct evaluation with users.

Integrated Bose audio · www.xreal.com
Integrated Bose audio

Keep feedback close to the wearer

The product integrates audio tuned with Bose rather than relying solely on a separate listening device. Locating sound near the viewing interface can reinforce media cues and reduce the number of accessories needed for a session. For shared or noisy environments, that integration raises design questions about intelligibility and disturbance, which cannot be answered by the audio partnership name alone.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

Tune the experience without multiplying controls

Movie presentation mode · www.xreal.com
Movie presentation mode

Give imagery an explicit viewing intent

Movie mode is one of the named display presets alongside Standard and Reading. A small set of recognizable intents lets the system tune presentation without making users manage every underlying image parameter. The aesthetic value lies in controlled adaptation: content can feel appropriate to its task while the physical object and its interaction vocabulary remain restrained.

Reading presentation mode · www.xreal.com
Reading presentation mode

Make typography a distinct viewing task

The Reading mode example emphasizes dense text rather than cinematic imagery or immersive gameplay. Treating reading separately acknowledges that character clarity and sustained scanning are different demands from dramatic color or motion. The lesson is to define a display mode around a recognizable information task, then judge whether its typography and contrast support that task rather than rewarding visual intensity.

Personal viewing envelope · www.xreal.com
Personal viewing envelope

Design the surrounding visual boundary

The entertainment scene depicts a large perceived screen occupying a private viewing envelope. Its relationship to the surrounding room helps communicate the product's spatial-display proposition more directly than a hardware specification. This is an official experience visualization, not proof of a measured viewing outcome; its design value is showing how content, setting and user attention are intended to work together.

Controller continuity · www.xreal.com
Controller continuity

Preserve familiar actions around new displays

The gaming scene pairs the wearable display with a conventional handheld controller. That continuity lets the display change substantially while the user's established input vocabulary remains recognizable. The interaction lesson is that a new presentation surface need not require simultaneous reinvention of every action, when reliable physical controls already support fast, learned responses during an intensive activity.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Test the whole viewing loop

Reference for check spatial stability01

Check spatial stability

Prototype anchored task information around a repeatable working posture rather than a spectacular virtual-screen size. Measure drift, reacquisition time and head movement during actual scanning sequences before selecting a spatial-display configuration.

Reference for expose useful contrast choices02

Expose useful contrast choices

Offer task-specific visibility settings when ambient light competes with information in a wearable display. Compare reading accuracy and scene awareness under changing illumination, using the dimming example as a mechanism reference.

Reference for retain a dependable input path03

Retain a dependable input path

Explore hands-free interaction while retaining a physical fallback for unreliable gesture recognition or occupied hands. Evaluate recovery time and accidental activation during representative glove, motion and obstruction conditions in the intended workflow.

Reference for protect optical alignment04

Protect optical alignment

Before planning replaceable optical or display components, define how alignment is recovered after assembly or service. Measure repeatability and image placement across repeated builds rather than assuming module replacement preserves the viewing experience.

06 · Key Takeaway

A brighter screen still depends on a steadier fit.

XREAL combines brighter imagery with a revised nose interface and adjustable wearing geometry. The transferable design task is to evaluate sensing, display behavior and body contact together. For Zebra wearables, optical specifications should be judged alongside alignment retention, setup time and task interruption.
