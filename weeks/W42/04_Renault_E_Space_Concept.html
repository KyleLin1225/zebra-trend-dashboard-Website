Renault E-Space Concept

E-Space begins with the people and activities inside the cabin. Reconfigurable seats, separated display and input zones, and a visually light enclosure make spatial organization the central design proposition.

01 · Design Highlights

Begin with the people inside

MPV envelope · media.renault.com
MPV envelope

Put interior volume before visual aggression

E-Space uses a low-waisted MPV envelope instead of adopting the dominant high-bodied SUV silhouette. Its short overhangs and expansive glazing make the cabin the visible center of the composition. The consequence is a clearer relationship between exterior form and shared interior space, presenting useful volume as a positive product identity rather than hiding it behind a more aggressive vehicle archetype.

Glazed cabin boundary · media.renault.com
Glazed cabin boundary

Keep the passenger volume visually open

Renault describes a glazed cabin whose windows extend visually behind portions of the body panels. That layering softens the apparent boundary between metal enclosure and occupied interior space. The resulting lightness comes from a readable construction relationship, not just a thin black graphic; it suggests how enclosure design can preserve awareness of the people and activities inside a compact footprint.

Technical baseplate · media.renault.com
Technical baseplate

Give support systems a separate visual layer

A dark lower band gathers the concept's technical base beneath the red passenger volume. Separating this support layer reduces the apparent bulk of the body while suggesting where battery and aerodynamic systems belong. The visual organization makes two priorities legible at once: an approachable occupied space above and a more technical foundation below, without requiring decorative complexity across the object.

Six independent seats · media.renault.com
Six independent seats

Make access part of reconfiguration

The concept places six individual seats on a flat floor and allows controlled changes in their orientation. Rotating the seating relationship changes access and the social arrangement rather than increasing the number of adjustment features. This makes spatial flexibility understandable activities, although a concept's inviting layout does not establish the force, locking feedback or durability of a production mechanism.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Separate information from physical reach

Shared table configuration · media.renault.com
Shared table configuration

Design the transition between activities

Renault's seating proposal can form a shared table arrangement within the passenger compartment. Changing the relationship between seats and surfaces supports another activity within the same cabin. The system lesson is to consider transition states as carefully as final layouts: users need to know what moves, what locks and which arrangement is appropriate for the next stage of use.

Corner-positioned wheels · media.renault.com
Corner-positioned wheels

Let packaging organize the usable center

The concept's large wheels sit near the corners of a short-overhang body. This emphasizes a broad central passenger volume and gives the MPV a planted visual stance. For design analysis, the important relationship is between the support footprint and the activities it surrounds; the wheel graphic alone cannot establish packaging efficiency, but it makes the spatial priority easy to perceive.

Separated display and input · media.renault.com
Separated display and input

Position information separately from touch

OpenR Panorama spans the cabin while separate touch controls sit closer to the driver. Renault distinguishes the distant information surface from the reachable input area instead of expecting users to touch the panoramic display. This architecture directly connects sightline and reach decisions, showing how a large display can remain accessible without forcing the same location to satisfy every interaction requirement.

Open access architecture · media.renault.com
Open access architecture

Evaluate the whole entry sequence

The concept's opposing doors open broad side access without a central pillar. That arrangement makes the relationship between outside approach, seating and the flat floor visible. Its design value is the complete entry sequence, while stiffness, sealing and crash performance remain separate engineering questions; the open concept image should not be read as proof that those tradeoffs have been resolved.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

Use material contrast to organize space

Front light signature · media.renault.com
Front light signature

Use one continuous identifying gesture

The front graphic combines a broad illuminated outline with the central Renault identity. A controlled light gesture organizes a calm surface instead of distributing attention across many competing details. The result is a recognizable face whose apparent technical complexity stays concentrated in one zone, illustrating how a strong visual hierarchy can create character without covering every panel with separate signatures.

Rear light perimeter · media.renault.com
Rear light perimeter

Repeat the system's grammar at the rear

The rear light treatment frames the tail with a graphic relationship to the front signature. This connects opposite ends of the vehicle through a common visual rule without copying every shape. The resulting continuity helps the product read as one system from different approaches, while the large glazed area remains a separate, legible expression of the occupied cabin volume.

Soft contact surfaces · media.renault.com
Soft contact surfaces

Use touch to balance the screen architecture

Textile-covered interior surfaces sit alongside harder metallic controls and extensive display technology. That contrast gives human-contact areas a warmer character without requiring the technical interface to disappear. The material strategy is because it assigns visual and tactile expectations to different zones; its enterprise transfer would require materials and cleaning performance rather than direct adoption of the concept's soft finishes.

Coordinated red environment · media.renault.com
Coordinated red environment

Make color a spatial organizer

The cabin uses related red tones across seats, surfaces and surrounding trim. Varying material and brightness within a controlled color family lets depth and contact zones remain readable inside a strongly unified interior. The aesthetic lesson is that color can organize an environment rather than merely decorate isolated parts, provided functional differentiation survives the desire for a coherent overall mood.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Make working space deliberately configurable

Reference for separate viewing from reaching01

Separate viewing from reaching

Place shared status information where it is visible, then position frequent controls within comfortable reach. Compare glance transitions, reach distance and posture across standing and seated workstation tasks before fixing the display and input locations.

Reference for plan the complete footprint02

Plan the complete footprint

Map operator movement, hand clearance and accessory access before styling a workstation enclosure or vehicle mount. Use full-size layout studies to check whether the proposed arrangement preserves access during task changes and maintenance.

Reference for check approach from every side03

Check approach from every side

Review workstation visibility and access from the routes operators actually use, including oblique approaches. Test whether labels, release points and controls remain discoverable without requiring users to move into a single ideal viewing position.

Reference for keep system identity coherent04

Keep system identity coherent

Develop one identity rule across a terminal, its mount and nearby accessories while preserving functional distinctions. Review recognition at working distance so a shared appearance helps users find the right component without obscuring state information.

06 · Key Takeaway

Useful space is an interaction architecture.

E-Space turns seating, storage, controls and sightlines into one connected proposal. For Zebra, the relevant transfer is to plan operator movement, mounting and shared information together. The concept is a design study; its materials and mechanisms suggest directions for prototypes rather than validated enterprise solutions.
