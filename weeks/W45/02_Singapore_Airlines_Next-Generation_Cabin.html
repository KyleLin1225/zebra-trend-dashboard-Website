Singapore Airlines Next-Generation Cabin

Two single First Class suites drop their divider and become an 80 by 60 inch double bed, and the seat position you set is carried forward to your next flight. The cabin's unit of design is no longer the seat — it is the setting.

01 · Design Highlights

A cabin planned around a curve, not a grid.

PriestmanGoode cabin plan · via Mainly Miles
FLOWING AISLE PLAN

The aisle drawn as a curve

PriestmanGoode's plan sketch annotates the walkway twice as a flowing aisle, and the suites are set against that curve rather than against a straight corridor. Starting from circulation instead of from seat count is unusual in a cabin with only six units to place.

Curved First Class aisle · Singapore Airlines via Mainly Miles
LOUVRED SUITE DOORS

Louvres instead of a flat door

Built out, the curve reads as a short street of louvred doors rather than a corridor of blank panels. Slatting a door you walk past at arm's length keeps the cabin feeling occupied, which is the opposite problem to the one a solid door solves.

Divider mid-travel · Singapore Airlines via Mainly Miles
RETRACTABLE DIVIDER

A wall photographed half-way

Singapore Airlines chose to publish the divider mid-travel rather than up or down. Showing the transitional state is a deliberate editorial act: it says the partition is a control the passenger operates, not a fixed piece of the aircraft's architecture.

Centre pair as a double bed · Singapore Airlines via Mainly Miles
80 BY 60 INCH BED

Two suites resolved into one room

With the divider down and the doors shut, the centre pair makes an 80 by 60 inch bed inside a sealed enclosure. Each single bed is 80 by 30, so the second dimension doubles exactly — the geometry was set by the pair, then divided.

02 · Technology / Product Architecture

Built as states to select, not settings to adjust.

Seat preset screen and vanity drawer, mock-up · Executive Traveller
NAMED SEAT PRESETS

Five named states on the screen

The 32-inch screen offers takeoff and landing, bed, dine, lounge and vanity mirror as named destinations rather than a recline slider. Choosing between situations instead of angles collapses a continuous adjustment into five decisions a tired passenger can actually make.

Active panel detail, mock-up · Executive Traveller
ALWAYS-ON ACTIVE PANEL

A second surface that never sleeps

A separate panel holds time remaining, both cities' local times and temperatures, the lavatory state and the seat number. Splitting ambient status onto its own always-on surface means the main screen can be given over to content without hiding the facts.

Suite side panel loom on the jig · Collins Aerospace via Executive Traveller
SIDE PANEL WIRING LOOM

The loom laid out on a jig

Collins Aerospace builds the suite's side panel around a wiring loom laid up flat on a jig before the panel exists. Every lit surface, motor and screen in that console is an entry in this harness, which is the real constraint on where a feature can go.

Business Class cabin mock-up at the launch · The Points Guy
CABIN SHOWN AS MOCK-UP

A full cabin built before it flies

Singapore Airlines showed Business Class as a walk-in mock-up, openly unfinished in places, more than a year before service entry. Committing floor space to a full-scale rig is how a cabin's circulation and sightlines get judged at the only scale that tells the truth.

03 · Aesthetic Analysis

One motif, carried across four cabins.

Business Class seat · RECARO Aircraft Seating
PERFORATED QUILT MOTIF

A pattern punched, not printed

The taupe leather carries its motif as perforation rather than as print or embroidery, with a single yellow line running the headrest. Perforation survives wear in a way a printed face does not, so the decoration and the breathability are the same decision.

Cocktail tray recess · RECARO Aircraft Seating
LIT STOWAGE RECESS

A tray lined and lit from above

The cocktail recess gets its own downwash lighting and a printed liner of stylised bird-and-aircraft silhouettes. Lighting a single stowage well rather than the whole console is what lets a dark cabin stay dark while one small working surface remains usable at night.

Centre suites partitioned, mock-up · Executive Traveller
EMBOSSED BATIK WALL

Batik held in relief, not colour

Partitioned, the suite shows a wall panel carrying the batik motif as embossing above a rust-red dado band. Reading the pattern as a shadow rather than a colour lets the same identity survive the cabin's full lighting range, including the dimmed cruise scenes.

Premium Economy row · Singapore Airlines via Mainly Miles
CABIN-CODED COLOURWAY

The same motif, a different key

Premium Economy takes slate blue with an orange shoulder flash and keeps the batik on the cushion. Running one motif through four different colourways is how a four-cabin aircraft reads as one aeroplane while still telling a passenger which cabin they are in.

05 · What Zebra Can Apply

Let the setting travel with the person.

Star memory button · Singapore Airlines via Mainly Miles01

Carry the fit, not the login

Identity Guardian already carries a worker's language, brightness and volume between shared devices. Add handedness, grip and trigger sensitivity to that set, then measure how long first-scan setup takes at shift start.

Economy seatback with secondary strip · Singapore Airlines via Mainly Miles02

Give status its own strip

Zebra's wearables report state through two to four RGB LEDs. Try a narrow always-on strip naming the active profile and battery state in words, then count how often the main screen gets woken.

Seat actuation on the test rig · RECARO via Mainly Miles03

Specify how it sounds

RECARO developed and patented the seat's actuation to be noiseless. Zebra publishes sound only as beeper and speaker output; set a target for the latch, trigger and cradle, then test it on a night shift.

Annotated side console concept · PriestmanGoode via Executive Traveller04

Publish the surface map

Every zone of this console is drawn and named. Zebra designs to VESA, RAM and AMPS but publishes no device CAD; release a dimensioned zone map per device, then see what partners build against it.

06 · Key Takeaway

The setting is the product, not the seat.

Singapore Airlines designs states rather than parts: centre suites that de-partition into one bed, five named seat situations instead of a recline angle, and preferences that persist to the next journey. Zebra already carries a worker's language, display and sound between shared devices; the gap is handedness, grip and trigger sensitivity.
